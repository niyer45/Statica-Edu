# Fatigue Analysis I — scoped requirements (task 70)

Additive specification, 2026-10-19. A new challenge requested by the owner on 2026-10-19 ("Fatigue Analysis Challenge (Advanced)"); decision D-60 records the owner-requested activation. It follows the shared contracts in `../multi-model-architecture/` (MM-01–MM-27) and the presentation decisions D-26, D-45, D-47–D-52 and D-56. It does not change any other challenge. Requirement IDs are `FAT-nn`. design.md holds the model, the sources, the worksheet and the verification plan; `fatigue_model.py` and `worksheet.py` are the standalone script the worksheet numbers come from.

Status: specified, implemented and registered by task 70. Owner engineering review (29.2) of the correlations, constants, thresholds and wording is still required before any customer release. The correlations are educational screening estimates, not a certification method.

## Identity and learning objectives

- FAT-01 Identity: challenge `fatigue-analysis-1`, version 1, title Fatigue Analysis I, difficulty advanced, discipline Mechanics, Guided and Parametric modes. Model `steppedShaftFatigue` version 1 (a new id, MM-01). Catalog card 009, available, technical-art kind `fatigue`, description "Keep a stepped axle spindle alive for its target life under a fluctuating load.", time 35–45 min, concepts Fatigue and Stress concentrations.
- FAT-02 Learning objectives: (a) fatigue is driven by the stress amplitude at a stress raiser, so the local stress Kf·σ, not the nominal stress, sets the life; (b) the shoulder fillet and the surface finish matter as much as the size of the section; (c) the endurance limit (infinite life) differs from the fatigue strength at a finite target life, and a mean stress lowers the amplitude a part can carry (modified Goodman). The guidance must say that these are screening correlations with scatter, not guarantees.

## Component, fixed values and adjustable design

- FAT-03 Component: a non-rotating steel stub-axle spindle, held rigidly by its collar (diameter D, length 40 mm). The neck (diameter d, length 60 mm) carries a wheel load 50 mm from the shoulder; the shoulder between collar and neck has a fillet of radius r. The load fluctuates between 1 kN and 5 kN every cycle (mean 3 kN, amplitude 2 kN), so the bending stress at the fillet has a mean and an alternating part.
- FAT-04 Fixed (locked): load (mean 3000 N, amplitude 2000 N), load arm 50 mm, collar and neck lengths, material "Steel (educational nominal, cold-drawn medium-carbon)": ultimate strength 630 MPa, yield strength 530 MPa, density 7850 kg/m³; Marin load, temperature and miscellaneous factors 1, reliability 90 % (factor 0.897); target life 2×10⁵ cycles. Locked fields: `materialId`, `load`, `geometry` (lengths and arm), `support`.
- FAT-05 Adjustable: neck diameter d 20–40 mm (0.5 mm grid), collar diameter D 30–60 mm (0.5 mm grid), fillet radius r 0.5–5 mm (0.25 mm grid), surface finish at the fillet: ground, machined, or as-forged. A design is geometrically valid only if r is smaller than the shoulder height (D − d)/2 (the fillet must fit inside the step); otherwise the edit is rejected and the draft is kept (MM-09).
- FAT-06 Initial design (Baseline): d 22 mm, D 45 mm, r 1 mm, as-forged. Internal reference, never shown to students: d 24.5 mm, D 34 mm, r 2 mm, machined. The reference is one feasible design among many (the worksheet grid has 270 Accepted designs).

## Model, metrics and checks

- FAT-07 Model (design.md): Roark polynomial fit for Kt of a shoulder fillet in bending; Neuber notch sensitivity with Shigley's Neuber-constant fit for steel; Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1) applied to both the mean and the alternating nominal stress; endurance limit Se = ka·kb·kc·kd·ke·Se′ with Se′ = 0.5 Sut, Shigley surface factor ka = a·Sut^b and size factor kb with the non-rotating equivalent diameter 0.370 d; Basquin line from f·Sut at 10³ cycles to Se at 10⁶ cycles; modified Goodman for the mean stress; first-cycle yield with Kf.
- FAT-08 Displayed metrics: fatigue safety factor at the target life, first-cycle yield safety factor, Kt, q, Kf, nominal and local alternating and mean stress, ka, kb, Se, the fatigue strength at the target life, the equivalent completely reversed stress, the predicted life (a number of cycles in the finite-life regime, "infinite (below the endurance limit)" otherwise), shoulder face, mass.
- FAT-09 Checks, in order: `fatigueSafetyFactor` ≥ 1.3 (modified Goodman at the target life), `yieldSafetyFactor` ≥ 1.5 (first cycle, Kf applied), `mass` ≤ 0.52 kg, `shoulderFace` ≥ 2.5 mm (flat shoulder face (D − d)/2 − r for the wheel hub to seat against), `applicability`. Lower bounds use an inclusive mirrored tolerance; a value equal to its limit passes. Accepted iff applicable and every check passes.
- FAT-10 Prediction: checkpoints on iterations 1 and 3; the primary metric is the fatigue safety factor, named "fatigue safety factor"; ratio band 0.98/1.02 (MM-13).
- FAT-11 Reflection prompt: "Using your tested designs, explain how the fillet radius, the surface finish and the neck diameter each changed the fatigue safety factor, and why making the neck thicker alone was not the answer."

## Applicability and exclusions

- FAT-12 Screens (not applicable above 1, inclusive, REL_TOL; no warning band): `KT_FIT_RANGE` (largest of (h/r)/20, 0.1/(h/r), (r/d)/0.3, (D/d)/3, the range of the Kt fit and of the chart it reproduces); `LOCAL_YIELD` (Kf·(σa + σm)/Sy: the notch yields on the first cycle, so the elastic Kf and the Goodman line no longer apply); `LOW_CYCLE` (σa/(f·Sut) + σm/Sut: the predicted life would be below 10³ cycles, outside the stress-life method). With the configured material and load ratio `LOCAL_YIELD` always fires before `LOW_CYCLE`; the latter is defense in depth and is tested with a modified challenge.
- FAT-13 Loader rules guarantee the correlations' own ranges for every configured value: ultimate strength within the Neuber-constant fit (340–1700 MPa) and the f fit (70–200 kpsi, about 483–1379 MPa), Sut ≤ 1400 MPa for Se′ = 0.5 Sut, the equivalent diameter 0.370 d within the size-factor range (2.79–51 mm), the target life within 10³–10⁶ cycles, yield below ultimate, and positive ranges.
- FAT-14 Always-listed unsupported effects: corrosion and fretting (at the hub fit), temperature, residual stresses from forming, rolling or shot peening, variable-amplitude loading and cumulative damage, notch-root plasticity and crack growth (screened only), scatter beyond the fixed reliability factor, the fillet's own volume and the hub's stiffening of the fillet, shear from the transverse load, and stress raisers other than the shoulder (keyways, threads, holes).
